# Editorial illustration style guide

This is the current default for future TWK shorts. Legacy cartoon/3D settings are retained in source for existing episodes, not mandatory design rules.

## Visual language

- Paper `#f1eddd`, dark ink `#26352f`, red `#cb4a32`, muted green `#467765` are the Boeing pilot palette. Adapt deliberately for a new topic rather than cycling colors automatically.
- A serif editorial headline contrasts with clear sans-serif annotations and tabular digits. One headline, one dominant visual action, one supporting annotation per composition where possible.
- Physical form is specific: fasteners, reels, cutaways, silhouettes and movement have a reason. Texture is quiet and does not hide bad geometry.
- Use original SVG/drawing primitives or cleared assets. Do not embed another channel's artwork or copy its exact compositions.
- Repeated parts are appropriate for a repeated mechanism, not as a substitute for shot design.

## Editing language

Start with the interesting contradiction. Cut on a new idea or an action, not a metronome. Carry a recognizable object or number across cuts. Use a short anticipation and a readable settled state. The result must be understandable when watched without sound. A held drawing is allowed when it supports comprehension; meaningless camera creep is not a quality rule.

## Text and mobile layout

Caption theme defaults to `editorial` in new-episode creation. Phrase pages are short, stable and readable. Active-word red is enough; no constant bounce, thick outlined white text or karaoke fireworks. Keep the bottom 17% clear for captions/UI, and review the result at phone size. Diagram labels and captions must not collide.

## Truth in pictures

A number drum is an analogy, not a claim that a GCU contains physical reels. A branch connector indicates shared logic, not aircraft wiring. A drawn document is labeled as an illustration. Do not invent technical internals just to make an image look detailed.

## Proof required

Inspect a complete rough cut, individual transitions, caption line breaks, critical labels and the final numerical state. Listen to the actual new voice take and final mix. Unit tests and a single still do not prove taste, performance or editorial accuracy. See `docs/CONTENT_SYSTEM.md` for the full workflow and `docs/RELEASE_CHECKLIST.md` for release gates.
